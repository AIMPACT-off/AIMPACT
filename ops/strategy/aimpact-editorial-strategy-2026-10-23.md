# AIMPACT Editorial Strategy — High-End Curiosity, Evidence, and Conversion
Date: 2026-10-23
Status: Editorial direction committed; audience performance hypotheses require testing against actual analytics.

## Executive decision
Do not publish generic “AI is changing the world” explainers as the default. Build an editorial portfolio around high-intent human questions: healthspan and longevity; parenting and child development; investing and funds; premium beauty, aesthetics and fitness; fine wine and spirits; high-end watches and the histories, craft, status signals, and collector preferences behind brands.

Every article must earn the click with a specific unresolved question, deliver credible evidence, explain competing interpretations, and end with a useful decision framework. Luxury content should explain provenance and preference rather than merely list expensive products. Health and finance content must distinguish evidence from claims and avoid individualized medical or investment advice.

## Audience and content portfolio hypothesis
These are strategic hypotheses to validate, not claims of measured AIMPACT audience performance.

## Cross-pillar editorial scorecard
Score each candidate 1–5, then apply weights: audience intent 25%, curiosity / distinctiveness 20%, evidence availability 20%, search longevity 15%, visual storytelling 10%, conversion relevance 10%. Do not call a topic a winner until search, click, retention, saves, qualified inquiries, and conversion data support it.

## Required article structure
1. **Title:** one sharp question or counterintuitive tension; no empty superlatives.
2. **Opening:** a concrete dilemma, surprising historical fact, or common misconception.
3. **Evidence:** primary sources first; date-stamp market/medical facts.
4. **Why people prefer it:** psychology, identity, craft, convenience, social signaling, risk perception, or actual performance—separate plausible explanation from proven fact.
5. **Counterpoint:** what critics, evidence, or competing products suggest.
6. **Decision guide:** what readers should check before acting or buying.
7. **Visuals:** one original hero image plus informative diagrams or comparison visuals; no decorative image with no explanatory value.
8. **Sources:** direct links placed next to claims and a compact source list.
9. **CTA:** relevant and transparent; no fake urgency.
10. **Measurement:** record URL, impressions, CTR, average reading time, saves/comments, outbound clicks, qualified leads, conversions.

## Competitive research and measurement protocol
Before selecting each topic:
- Review Google Trends and search suggestions for the relevant language/market.
- Inspect top-ranking results and identify what they fail to answer.
- Review audience discussions (including Reddit where relevant) for recurring questions, confusion, and counterarguments.
- Prefer official/primary sources: peer-reviewed studies and public health bodies; fund prospectuses, exchange filings and issuer reports; brand archives and technical documents; appellation/regulatory bodies and reputable auction data.
- Compare at least three independent evidence types when the claim is consequential.
- Track a 30-day baseline and compare performance by pillar. Small samples are directional, not proof.
- Do not assert that a pillar “performs best” until actual analytics are available.

## Immediate operating rule
Create one complete, source-backed article per selected pillar before increasing volume. Publish only after claim review, visual review, and platform permission checks. Report separately: draft saved, scheduled, published URL verified, analytics observed.
